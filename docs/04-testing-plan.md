# Epistrel — Testing Plan

**Full-scope test strategy · Working draft**

This plan describes how Epistrel is tested for correctness, quality, efficiency, and robustness, and how its Orchestrator behavior is challenged against real-world narrative scenarios. It references requirement IDs from *03 — Requirements Specification*.

---

## 1. Testing philosophy

- **Separate Engine Core correctness from model quality.** The Engine Core is deterministic and model-free (NFR-DETERM). It is tested with a **stub model adapter** (fixed responses) so logic is verified with ordinary assertions and never flakes on model nondeterminism. Model-dependent behavior is tested separately as *evaluations* with statistical gates.
- **Invariants over examples.** The properties that matter most (private thoughts never enter another character's context; projections equal the event-fold; regen preserves committed facts) are universal and get **property-based tests**, not a handful of examples.
- **Challenge the behavior, not just the units.** A **scenario harness** drives scripted stories end-to-end through the Orchestrator and asserts story-state outcomes — where the whitepaper's scenarios become executable tests.

Quality gates (coverage, eval floors, latency SLOs, determinism must-pass) are enforced in CI; see §13.

## 3. Unit tests (deterministic Engine Core)

Fast, stub model, coverage ≥ 90% of core logic.

- **[U-01] Commitment lifecycle (FR-COMMIT-1,2,3,4,5,6,7):** **[M4: FR-COMMIT-1..3,5..7]** thunk vs. committed transitions; consequence-driven commit fires exactly when a `think`/`do` depends on a value; salience boundary (texture vs. signal) with scripted salience verdicts; a global thunk has no world value yet; promissory ledger blocks turn close on dangling references. **[M5: FR-COMMIT-4,5]** Determined-but-unknown (a committed world fact unknown to a given character) is distinct from a global thunk; committing a salient-but-hidden fact records the noticing character's observation in their partition and the truth in canon, scoped away until revealed.
- **[U-02] Coordinate logic (FR-BITEMP-1,2,3,4,5):** narrative position, valid-time, and transaction-time queried independently; supersede closes transaction-time, never deletes; retcon spans; truncation never alters valid-time; membership flips are reversible.
- **[U-03] Epistemic model (FR-EPI-1,2,3,4,5,6, FR-BELIEF-15):** self/social/nested belief storage and query — nested records carry holder, attributed holder, inner proposition, attributed and own confidence, provenance, and the three times, to depth 2 by default and to a configured depth 3; a belief about a speech act (`said`) is stored distinctly from a belief about a belief; false belief as character-truth; could-have-known gating.
- **[U-04] Verification set-logic and canonization (FR-VERIFY-1..4):** **[M4: FR-PROP-1, FR-VERIFY-1,3,4]** supported/contradicted/novel/uncertain over normalized propositions with no model call; a permitted `novel` verdict canonizes with provenance and visibility. **[M5: FR-VERIFY-2]** Viewpoint selection.
- **[U-15] Contradiction rules, one at a time (FR-PROP-1,2,8,9,10,11,12):** **[M4: FR-PROP-1,2,8..12]** a fixture per rule — same proposition/same polarity with the claim's interval inside canon's (Ohio 2010–2014 vs claim 2011–2013) → supported; **coverage counterexample:** canon Ohio 2010–2014, claim Ohio 2000–2020 → *not* supported but uncertain(`partial_coverage`) with covered [2010, 2014) and uncovered [2000, 2010) ∪ [2014, 2020) reported, and the remainder evaluated as a separate novel claim; two adjacent canon intervals 2010–2012 and 2012–2014 jointly cover a 2010–2014 claim → supported; an instant event at t with start ≤ t < end → covered, at t = end → not covered; **mixed granularity:** canon "May 10 09:00" supports a claim of "May 10"; canon "May 10" does *not* support a claim of "May 10 09:00" (uncertain, `granularity_refinement`, the hour treated as a novel detail); for a unique event (birth) canon "May 10 09:00" vs claim "May 10 17:00" → contradicted, vs claim "May 10" → compatible, vs "May 11" → contradicted; two non-unique knock events at "May 10 09:00" and "May 10 17:00" → two events, no contradiction, and a claim "someone knocked on May 10" is compatible with both but identifies neither; no `[t, t)` interval is ever constructed (rejected at normalization); opposite polarity overlapping → contradicted; functional with the same uniqueness key and different objects overlapping → contradicted, but Columbus vs Ohio (containment) → not contradicted; **key scoping:** `degree_awarded(Bobby, deg_A)` by {OSU} and `degree_awarded(Bobby, deg_B)` by {Florida} → no conflict (different keys), while deg_A by {Florida} → contradicted; a jointly awarded degree with set {OSU, Florida} is supported by a claim of that set and does not conflict with a second degree; a non-key qualifier (field of study) differing does not create a key conflict; a new member of a declared closed set over overlapping time → contradicted, and the same claim with no closed set → novel; multi-valued new object → novel, and beyond a declared maximum → contradicted; `alive`/`dead` exclusion overlapping → contradicted; negating an implied proposition under an **author-declared** rule in the fixture (`member_of(p, varsity team of X)` ⇒ `enrolled_at(p, X)`) → contradicted, and the same claim without the declared rule → novel; **weak predicate:** auto-registered `apprenticed_with(Bobby, blacksmith_1)` affirmed vs negated over overlapping time → contradicted (direct polarity), while a second apprenticeship with a different smith → novel; non-overlapping fluent values (moved cities) → no contradiction; approximate bounds with undecidable overlap → uncertain; approximate claim bounds that might extend past canon's inner bounds → not supported (uncertain); unregistered predicate or unnormalizable claim → uncertain; absence of any record → novel (open world); "last week" resolves against the claim's position; implication closure stops at the configured depth; the same proposition shape works for canon and utterance holders. **[M5: FR-PROP-2]** It works for belief holders too.
- **[U-16] Ledger granularity (FR-COMMIT-6,13):** **[M4: FR-COMMIT-13]** obligations are tracked per proposition: committing "a knock occurred" discharges the ledger entry for that proposition while "who knocked" and "why" remain open thunks; those sub-propositions become obligations when made salient themselves; a turn closes with them open. **[M5: FR-COMMIT-6,13]** With per-character partitions, they also become obligations when the knocker is a modeled character acting with intent, committed on the knocker's side.
- **[U-17] Perception qualifier validation (FR-TURN-13, FR-TURN-9,10):** a widening qualifier (eavesdrop, remote listening) is accepted only with a plausible perception path in the recorded positions and barriers, or an established channel (an active call); an eavesdrop across a sealed vault or from another city is rejected and repaired; narrowing qualifiers (whisper, foreign language) are accepted without validation.
- **[U-18] Configuration classification (FR-CONC-14,15):** every setting in the configuration schema carries an `interpretive` or `prospective` tag; a schema test fails the build if one is missing; at runtime an untagged setting is treated as interpretive; every configuration write produces a new configuration revision.
- **[U-19] Resolution obligations (FR-COMMIT-14):** **[M4: FR-COMMIT-14]** binding a thunk to an entity derives the retroactive propositions the binding implies at the original valid-time (position at t) from declared predicate semantics, and the conflict search over those obligations finds a recorded contrary position. **[M5: FR-COMMIT-14]** It also derives the knowledge the binding implies (knowledge of the act from t on), and the conflict search finds a later sincere line inconsistent with that knowledge.
- **[U-20] Pacing rule (FR-CLOCK-6,7):** beats of 0, 4, 5, 50, 51, and 300 words in `say`+`do` advance 0, 0, 1, 1, 2, and 2 minutes; `think`, `latent`, and effect declarations add nothing; player input counts; sequential beats sum; simultaneous beats take the maximum; a beat with a declared or estimated skip uses the skip instead of its pacing increment; a custom per-story table (e.g., 0–9 → 0, 10–99 → 1, 100+ → 3) is applied exactly; no model call is made.
- **[U-21] Clock verification by speaker (FR-CLOCK-4,9,10,15):** with `time_awareness = exact` — **narration** "good evening" at 10:00 → contradicted, at 18:30 → supported; **Jenni** sincerely acting on her 17:00 meeting at 11:20 → belief-inconsistent, no deceptive intent → repaired, at 16:55 → accepted; Jenni saying "I have to run to my meeting" with a checked `think` that it's an excuse to leave → deceptive intent → accepted as a lie with a divergence recorded; "it's about 3:10" at 15:20 → consistent within ±15 min, at 15:40 → belief-inconsistent; "we've been here for hours" after 20 perceived minutes → inconsistent, after 2 h → consistent; a joking "it's practically midnight" at 16:00 → accepted as a joke; an early departure for an established errand → accepted. **Overrides:** Bobby, whose watch stopped at 15:00 (an observation recorded earlier), sincerely says "it's three" at 17:30 → belief-consistent, objectively false, accepted (a sincere error); Bobby says "it's three" with no such antecedent → hallucination, repaired. **Awareness levels:** under `approximate`, "about four" at 16:40 → consistent, "it's 4:52 exactly" with no timepiece → inconsistent; under `none`, "must be around noon" is accepted as a low-confidence guess and "it's 12:07" is not. **Perception gaps:** Marcus, unconscious from 20:00 to 23:00, has perceived no elapsed time for that span; "it's still eight" on waking is belief-consistent. Custom calendars and time-of-day bands are honored; an unanchored clock leaves absolute-time narration uncertain until anchored, then anchors on the first established time.
- **[U-22] Deterministic claim detectors (FR-VERIFY-13):** **[M4: FR-VERIFY-13]** each detector fires on a fixture embedded in otherwise valid prose with no declared proposition or effect — "She pockets the key on her way out" (possession), "that's forty silver, then" (quantity), "it's nearly midnight" (time), a mention of an entity not covered by any declared proposition, and a protected-content match on an undetermined thunk's possible value; every hit is turned into a claim and verified; detectors are deterministic and need no model call. **[M5: FR-VERIFY-13]** Protected-content matching also fires on the alias of a fact hidden from the speaker and on another character's private `think` phrase.
- **[U-23] Beat and step timing (FR-CLOCK-6,9,17):** clock 15:05; one beat "At 15:10 she starts driving; twenty minutes later she arrives" → step 1 at 15:10, `driving` over [15:10, 15:30), step 2 (arrival) at 15:30, beat end 15:30, no pacing increment added; a beat with no advances starts at the previous beat's end and ends after its pacing increment; a narrated "at 15:00" when the clock reads 15:05 → contradicted unless marked as a flashback; in a sequential turn, beat 2 starts at beat 1's end; in a simultaneous turn, all beats start at the turn start, one beat's 10-minute skip moves only its own steps, and the turn ends at the latest beat end; **pacing crosses an appointment:** a 60-word beat (2-minute increment, no skip) starting at 15:59 with a canon 16:00 meeting → the beat's end is clamped to 16:00, the beat is not regenerated, and a following sequential beat starts at 16:00 with the meeting surfaced as due; the same in a simultaneous turn starting at 15:59 → the turn end is clamped to 16:00 and the meeting is surfaced to the next turn; a pacing increment that reaches a `possibly_due` point pauses the same way; each claim, exposure, and belief acquisition carries its step's start time.
- **[U-25] Simultaneous cutoff with narrated consequences (FR-CLOCK-9,16,17,19, FR-MULTI-8):** a simultaneous turn starts at 14:50 with two beats: Jenni's beat says "I'm leaving at 15:10 — I can't miss my train" (her own new intention, so a canon `scheduled` event owned by her and involving a present character, FR-CLOCK-19), and Bobby's beat narrates "they wait fifty minutes; at 15:40 he finally gets his ticket and hands over 20 silver" (a skip, an acquisition, and a State effect after 15:10). **Ownership variant:** the 15:10 event is instead a gate closing — it is created by a world/director beat in the same turn, not by Jenni's beat (a character's statement that the gate closes at 15:10 is a report or an unpermitted world claim, never an owned plan); the cutoff behavior is identical. After reconciliation, assert: the cutoff is 15:10; Bobby's beat is regenerated under the cutoff, so no step, exposure, acquisition, or State effect is committed after 15:10 (the ticket and the 20 silver are not committed unless the regenerated beat places them before 15:10); Jenni's beat, which ends by pacing, keeps its end; every beat end ≤ the committed turn end = 15:10; Jenni's departure is surfaced to the next turn as due. A variant where Bobby's beat only overruns through its pacing increment is clamped without regeneration.
- **[U-24] Partial anchors (FR-CLOCK-3,10,18):** an unanchored clock at offset +130 min is anchored by "Saturday morning" → weekday Saturday, time in [05:00, 12:00) at that position, date unknown; the position 130 minutes earlier maps to [02:50, 09:50) Saturday — and a position 600 minutes earlier maps to a range crossing midnight, so its weekday is Friday-or-Saturday; "good morning" at the anchor → supported, "good evening" → contradicted, "it's 9:15" → refines the anchor to an exact minute and back-fills earlier positions exactly; "it's 13:00" at the anchor → contradicted; "it's the 14th" → uncertain until a date is established; no check ever resolves by choosing a value inside a range; with the anchor range [05:00, 12:00), a 10:00 meeting is `possibly_due` immediately (10:00 lies inside the range), becomes `due` only at the offset where the lower end reaches 10:00 (+300 min), and a 13:00 meeting becomes `possibly_due` only once the upper end passes 13:00 (+61 min).
- **[U-26] Scenario slots and the starting-cast limit (FR-SCN-3,4,5,6):** a scenario with roles in each fill mode — `open` accepts a catalog character or an ad-hoc definition, `default` keeps its character unless replaced, `locked` rejects any reassignment; a missing required slot, an ineligible choice (label, content-rating, or age-range filter), and a non-overridable override are each rejected without side effects. **Limit:** with the deployment default, 16 slots including player slots are accepted and 17 rejected; a scenario that sets 20 accepts 20; a creation request overriding the limit to 24 is accepted; with an operator ceiling of 20, both the scenario value and the request override are capped at 20. Characters introduced after genesis are not counted.
- **[U-27] Facts from the scenario, personality from the character (FR-SCN-7,8, FR-CAT-2, FR-PROP-10, FR-CLOCK-9):** **[M3: FR-SCN-7, FR-CAT-2]** **personality** — the catalog Linda is fiercely honest and the scenario's open Mother role says "a habitual liar" → Linda's honesty wins, validation and creation return a personality warning with code `personality_in_role`, the role and field, the exact text "a habitual liar", outcome `ignored` (yields to the character), and the rule "personality: character over scenario", and the creation warning also names Linda as the character whose personality applies; with `strict` set, the same creation is refused with `creation_conflicts` and the full report, and nothing is created; two different characters in the same role produce different dispositions in their seeded definitions. Under `lite`, a character's background flavor "only child" and the role seed "has a sister" are both rendered, the scenario's fact first with the instruction that scenario facts prevail; nothing is dropped, and the report lists the fact-merge check as `check_unavailable`. **[M4: FR-SCN-7, FR-PROP-10]** **World facts** — in a structured story, a character's background flavor says "only child" while the role seeds "has a sister" → the role wins and the losing fact is dropped entirely; a world seed overrides a conflicting role seed. **[M5: FR-SCN-8]** **Beliefs** — the Brother's seeded false belief that the house is not being sold is kept without any conflict; two seeds giving the Mother opposite beliefs about her husband's faithfulness → the higher source wins and is reported. **Secrets** — the world seeds "the house is being sold" as secret to the Father and a role seed gives the Mother the same secret → both are holders; the world seeds a fact as public while a role seeds it as secret → the world's audience wins and is reported. **Nested beliefs** — the Sister's seeded belief that the Brother knows about the sale is kept although he does not. **Seed attributes** — every seed carries provenance `seed`, a holder, an audience (`self`, specific roles, `acquaintances`, `perceivable`, `public`), a disclosure (`open`, `guarded`, `secret`), and valid-time before the story began unless stated; an `acquaintances` item reaches exactly the roles whose relationships say they know the holder; a `secret` item makes the holder's later denial classify as a deliberate lie, not a hallucination. **[M6: FR-SCN-8, FR-CLOCK-9]** **Scheduled events** — the world's 18:00 dinner against a role seed of a 19:00 dinner (same event) → world wins; the Mother's own 15:00 errand coexists.
- **[U-28] Profile validation and effective configuration (FR-PROF-2,3,4,5,6):** **[M3: FR-PROF-2..6]** the profile schema holds the modules delivered so far — the pipeline, the rolling summary and pinned memories, budgets, and models — and the Lite preset validates; a module or level the deployment has not delivered is rejected, naming it; the effective configuration is deployment defaults → profile version → story overrides, validated as a whole, and an override that makes it invalid is rejected; every setting in the profile schema is classified `behavioral` or `data-producing`, and a build-time test fails if one is unclassified. **[M4: FR-PROF-2,4]** Each declared dependency among the delivered modules is exercised by a fixture that violates it and is rejected with the missing dependency named — consolidation without episodic memory; contradiction checking with verification off; verification on a `lite` profile, which names the `structured` pipeline. **[M5: FR-PROF-4]** Nested beliefs without partitions; partitions with perception `none`; perception `co_location` without partitions. **[M6: FR-PROF-4]** Time-claim verification without the clock; the clock in a `structured` profile without commitment. **[M7: FR-PROF-3]** The Standard, Full, and Max presets validate.
- **[U-29] Rating inheritance and change classification (FR-RATE-1,2,5,6,7):** **[M2: FR-RATE-1,2]** with the seeded ratings, Everyone's effective guardrails are its own plus Teen's, Mature's, and Explicit's, each tagged with its source rating, and prompt guidance is not inherited; a rating inheriting the same policy by two paths applies it once; an inheritance cycle is rejected on save. **[M3: FR-RATE-5..7]** Change classification: Teen → Explicit is relaxing (subset); Explicit → Teen is tightening; with two custom ratings *Mature-Violence* and *Mature-Romance* that each have a policy the other lacks, a move either way is tightening. Looser-than: an Explicit character against an Everyone scenario is looser; an Everyone character against an Explicit scenario is not; display order is never consulted.
- **[U-30] Resolution levels (FR-COMMIT-9, FR-STORE-29):** committing that a drawer holds a letter from Marcus at resolution level `consequential` fixes that the letter is there and who wrote it, while its incidental detail (the paper, the handwriting) stays undetermined; a render that only mentions the letter resolves nothing; the first render whose `think` or `do` depends on the handwriting resolves it once, through resolution reconciliation (FR-COMMIT-14), and it then has one value; a later contradicting claim about the handwriting is caught; rewinding past the resolving turn makes the handwriting undetermined again while the letter and its author stay fixed.
- **[U-31] Context assembly by priority tier (FR-RET-7):** with a stub tokenizer and items in configured tiers (for example policy and rating guidance; the character's definition and pins; resolved entity profiles; ranked retrieved memories; recent messages), the helper includes tiers in priority order and items within a tier in rank order; it never exceeds the budget; items are dropped whole, never cut mid-item; a lower-tier item is included only when every higher-tier item is included or cannot fit in what remains; the result lists what was dropped; and the helper accepts only an already viewpoint-filtered set, so it cannot widen what a character sees (NFR-PRIV-4).
- **[U-05] Speech-act handling (FR-TURN-5..7):** **[M4: FR-TURN-5,6]** utterance stored without promotion to truth; objective relation and communicative intent derived independently. **[M5: FR-TURN-6,7]** The speaker's belief relation derived independently of both; divergence creation on lie.
- **[U-06] Mutation primitives (FR-GUARD-4):** quarantine excludes from retrieval; sanitize substitutes surrogate; canonize promotes with provenance.
- **[U-07] Directive classification and authority (FR-DIRECT-1,2,6):** ephemeral not persisted; durable applied; authority ordering.
- **[U-08] External-fact snapshot (FR-EXT-4):** committing a reference to a (stubbed) external value records an `external_read` event and the committed fact references it; later live drift does not change the recorded value.
- **[U-09] Rewrite mode disposition (FR-REWRITE-3,4,5,6):** over a stubbed blast radius, assert `synchronous`/`best_effort`/`threshold` dispositions and `preview_first`/`apply_directly` behave per spec; detection is identical across modes; the transaction is atomic and reverts wholesale on an induced mid-apply failure.
- **[U-10] Importance decay (FR-MEM-7)** and **retrieval ranking (FR-RET-5)** math on fixtures.
- **[U-11] Memory record shape (FR-MEM-1,2,4,5, FR-TURN-4):** episodic and semantic records carry provenance, trust tier, visibility, importance, source references, timestamps, and embedding-model version; working memory is derived from events and never separately persisted; `latent` routes to canon with an explicit visibility scope.
- **[U-12] Belief revision history (FR-BELIEF-10):** a correction preserves the prior belief bitemporally (the character can be shown to have been wrong, then corrected); forgetting lowers confidence/accessibility without contradicting the belief and is visible to consolidation.
- **[U-13] Policy store scopes (FR-GUARD-1,2,8):** no API path reachable by in-world content can mutate the policy store; policies are evaluated on every outbound message regardless of memory state; a story- or character-scoped policy cannot override a global safety policy.
- **[U-14] Owned-value declarations (FR-STATE-2):** story-owned and externally owned values share one declaration and verification path; the per-turn read, unavailability, and write policies apply only to externally owned values.

## 4. Integration tests (real datastore)

Against real Postgres+pgvector via **Testcontainers**, stub model, deterministic stub embedder (fixed hash-based vectors).

- **[I-01] Event store and atomic turn commit (FR-STORE-1,2,3,5, NFR-REL-1, FR-EXT-19):** **[M2: FR-STORE-1,2,5, NFR-REL-1]** events are append-only with a monotonic `seq`, typed payload, provenance, authority, valid-time and transaction-time; projections update in the same transaction; an ordinary turn is all-or-nothing, and an induced mid-commit failure rolls back fully. **[M4: FR-STORE-3]** The structured projections — relationships, placement, and inventory — update in the same transaction as the turn that changes them. **[M5: FR-STORE-3]** So do belief projections. **[M6: FR-EXT-19]** **Staged turn:** an induced failure inside any stage rolls back that stage only, leaving the turn at the previous stage boundary with earlier stages intact; the timeline revision advances exactly once at intent, once at outcome, and once at completion, and never at claim or response storage.
- **[I-02] Projection rebuild correctness (FR-STORE-4, FR-ADMIN-3):** incremental projection == fold of event log, across randomized sequences.
- **[I-03] Rewind/branch (FR-STORE-6, FR-STORE-20):** **[M2: FR-STORE-6]** state as of checkpoint N equals the corrected history through narrative position N (discarded records excluded), not the records with `seq` ≤ N; a background operation recorded after turn N but positioned earlier is included, and one positioned later is not; the branch is independent. **[M7: FR-STORE-6, FR-STORE-20]** Amendments that apply to N are included in the state as of N, including an amendment recorded after turn N that corrects earlier history.
- **[I-04] Truncate/fork/delete (FR-STORE-10..18, FR-STORE-20..23):** **[M2: FR-STORE-10..12,14..16,18]** in-story truncation marks records with narrative position > N discarded without deleting rows or touching valid-time; the corrected-history-through-N view returns the right records, and untruncation restores them; fork produces a full independent copy (no shared rows, no cross-story references) that diverges freely; whole-story delete removes only that story's rows. **[M4: FR-STORE-13]** A derived memory is excluded from the view through N when any of its sources is. **[M6: FR-STORE-17]** Recorded `external_read` events replay deterministically on the fork. **[M7: FR-STORE-20..23]** The as-originally-recorded view returns the right records, and amendments survive truncation.
- **[I-05] Resolution pipeline end-to-end (FR-RET-2,3,6):** "those guys a month ago" resolves time window → locates episode → resolves participants → returns scoped assessments.
- **[I-06] Hybrid retrieval (FR-RET-4):** vector + lexical returns exact proper-noun matches pure vector misses.
- **[I-07] Respond contract (FR-RESP-1,2,4,5,6):** the respond request returns a finished message; consumer does no memory work; scene context is used for continuity but never written as canon, and a distinctive phrase planted only in the consumer's recent messages appears in no captured generation prompt; multiple CharacterIDs compose into one message; an idempotency key and expected revision are accepted and the new timeline revision is returned, and a stale expected revision is rejected without side effects with the current revision.
- **[I-08] Migrations (Alembic) (NFR-REL-2):** forward/backward integrity; projection rebuild after schema change.
- **[I-09] Right-to-be-forgotten (FR-ADMIN-2, NFR-PRIV-2):** **[M3: FR-ADMIN-2, NFR-PRIV-2]** erase a subject; verify zero residue across events, projections, the transcript, and the rolling summary. **[M4: FR-ADMIN-2]** Zero residue across memories and vectors too. **[M5: FR-ADMIN-2]** And across other characters' beliefs.
- **[I-10] Story lifecycle; export and import (FR-ADMIN-1, FR-ADMIN-4, FR-STORE-7,30, FR-ADMIN-9, FR-RATE-7):** **[M2: FR-ADMIN-1]** create, archive, and delete stories and the characters defined in them; deleting a story removes its characters. **[M7: FR-ADMIN-4,9, FR-STORE-7,30, FR-RATE-7]** **Round trip:** a story with a truncated tail, a dormant continuation, an amendment, a staged turn with a confirmed outbox outcome, States, story audit events (a rejected render, a verification verdict, a repair attempt, and a rewrite question and answer), and feedback (a re-rated message and a rating on a regenerated variant) is exported and imported as a new story; with stub models, its corrected history at several checkpoints, as-originally-recorded view with those audit events, beliefs, States, ledger, turn variants, feedback — each rating with its principal, tags, text, rating history, and the turn and variant it rates, the regenerated variant's rating still on that variant — and the response to a fixed request equal the source's except for the story ID and owner; an imported audit event older than the target's audit retention is removed by the next retention sweep; rewind, branch, undo, and swap work on the imported story; the source is untouched. the attempt is marked `ambiguous`, settled at its horizon as presumed (`used = 2`, `basis = presumed`, cost `unknown`), and the job retries; the stalled worker then delivers one output with its cost → the record is corrected once (`basis = corrected`, `used = 1`, `overage = 0`, `extra_outputs = 0`, cost filled in), the story, turn, and deployment counters' `used` each fall by 1 while `reserved` is unchanged, the output is neither adopted nor promoted and its bytes are deleted, the attempt is reported `late_after_presumed`, and the retry's reservation and promotion are undisturbed; delivering the same result again changes nothing and is reported `late_duplicate`; a variant delivering three outputs for `requested = 2` → `used = 3`, `overage = 1`, `extra_outputs = 1`, counters rising by exactly those amounts; a variant in which the presumed usage ended the retry `cap_reached` stays ended after the correction; a stalled ambiguous attempt that delivers before its horizon settles `held → settled` as `late_discarded` and is never presumed; after every step the reconciler reports no drift; a result arriving after the accounting retention has purged its records is reported `late_unaccounted` with its cost recorded. **Submission modes:** with an `async` stub, `attempt_ref` is committed (`accepted`) before `collect` is called — a worker killed during `collect` leaves a known request, recovered through `status` when the stub declares `confirms_completion` and as a stalled attempt (held to its horizon, then the non-confirming rule) when it does not, never as an ambiguous send; a worker killed after `submit` is sent but before its acknowledgement is committed is ambiguous; with a `sync` stub, a worker killed during the call is ambiguous; a `sync` adapter declaring `confirms_completion` is rejected at registration; the bundled OpenRouter adapter reports `submission = sync` and `confirms_completion = false`. Counter locks are always taken in the order deployment → story → turn → entry, and a stress run with mixed scopes produces no deadlock. **Defaults:** a fresh deployment with an adapter configured has limits of 4 per turn, 40 per story per day, 16 per entry per day, and 200 per deployment per day, and catalog requests default to four candidates; a story override changes only that story's per-turn and per-story limits; a limit of 0 rejects requests at enqueue with `images_disabled`; configuration validation rejects a negative or missing limit, and no unlimited value is accepted; without an adapter, generation returns `not_configured`.
- **[I-19] Backfill (FR-PROF-8,9,10, FR-LITE-6):** a 1,000-message Lite story is switched to Full with `upgrade_mode = backfill`: the story is paused (respond returns `story_paused` with the backfill's progress; history reads still work); no transcript event is altered or regenerated; structured data is created with provenance `backfill` and the lower trust tier, positions and valid-times in narrative order, and inferred perception marked as inferred; a crash mid-backfill resumes from its last checkpoint; cancelling discards partial results and restores the Lite configuration. **Conflicts:** Jenni's car is "red" at turn 100 and "blue" at turn 400 with no repaint narrated → under `last_mention` canon is blue and turn 100 is recorded as an in-world claim; under `first_mention` canon is red; under `ask` the conflict is listed and the story stays paused until resolved, or resumes on `ignore_warnings` with the color `uncertain`; a car narrated as repainted at turn 250 is not a conflict; a story created without `backfill_conflict_policy` behaves as `last_mention`, and an upgrade request without `upgrade_mode` runs a backfill. **Downgrade/upgrade:** switching Full → Lite builds the rolling summary from the transcript and keeps structured data dormant; switching back after 50 more turns backfills only those 50.

## 6. Property-based tests (invariants)

Using **Hypothesis** over generated stories, characters, turns, and queries.

- **PRIV — private-thought non-leakage (NFR-PRIV-1,4, FR-MEM-11):** for any story and any retrieval for character A, no `think` memory owned by B≠A ever appears (minor characters included); no character's generation context contains content that character did not perceive (FR-MEM-11) or any consumer-supplied recent message; and no character's `think` is disclosed in player-facing output — verbatim, paraphrased, or as narration asserting an inner state recorded only in `think` — while inner state expressed through that character's own verified, perceived `say`/`do` is permitted. *The single most important invariant; tested exhaustively.*
- **CLOCK-MONOTONIC (FR-CLOCK-1,2,6,8,9,12,16,17,18):** for any sequence of turns, skips, regenerations, truncations, swaps, and forks: along the active timeline the clock never decreases; its value equals the fold of the recorded active advances; every beat's start and end time and every step's start time equal what FR-CLOCK-17 derives from the recorded advances — sequential beats chained end to start, simultaneous beats sharing the turn start, the turn ending at the committed clock value, which is the latest beat end — and every claim, exposure, and acquisition carries its step's start time; no accepted model-initiated skip exceeds `max_unprompted_skip` or lacks an established activity; with `stop_at_due_events` on, no advance carries the clock past a `pending` canon scheduled event involving a present character, no event stops the clock factually more than once without being rescheduled, no `possibly_due` pause occurs twice for one anchor state or is ever narrated as the event being due, no belief-only appointment ever stops it, and no committed record from a stopped beat has a story-time after the cutoff; replay reproduces the clock exactly from recorded advances with no estimation calls.
- **OUTPUT-VIEWPOINT (FR-RESP-8,12,13, FR-MULTI-4, FR-AUTH-2):** for any multi-character turn, positions, and character viewpoint V, every returned beat and every proposition in the composed narrative is perceivable by V at the returned fidelity (content only where V perceived content, envelope only where V perceived only the envelope, nothing where V perceived nothing); no `think` or `latent` content is returned; the composed narrative and the beat list agree; `omniscient` is returned only when the request names it, and a stored result is returned only for the viewpoint it was produced for (generation, idempotent replay, cache, or history read); the composition check enforces thought non-disclosure for character viewpoints and permits attributed, labeled thoughts only in explicitly requested omniscient output.
- **DETERMINED-SINGLE-VALUE (FR-COMMIT-6, FR-EPI-8, FR-PROP-3):** no determined proposition ever has more than one truth value at a given valid-time across all partitions, and no functional predicate ever has two objects for one **uniqueness key** over overlapping time (FR-PROP-3) — two distinct degrees held by one person over overlapping years are valid, two awarding sets for the same degree are not; multi-valued predicates may hold any number of true propositions; a character's "version" of a determined fact is either absence (unknown) or an owned belief record, never a competing truth. Assert that once a knocker is committed, no later message from an unknowing character can resolve a different knocker.
- **BELIEF-PROVENANCE (FR-EPI-7, FR-EPI-13, FR-BELIEF-2):** no belief exists without a recorded, typed acquisition cause; every belief-set transition traces to a permitted operation whose antecedent was independently verified and precedes the dependent step in (turn, beat, step) order (FR-EPI-14). Assert the belief set never changes without a permitted, antecedent-backed cause. **[M7: FR-VERIFY-10, FR-DIRECT-1]** (d) an **author directive** established the false belief → accepted.

Golden outputs are fixtures; diffs require explicit review.

## 8. Model-quality evaluations (model-dependent)

Use **real models**, report scores with variance, gate on thresholds. Run across multiple seeds and a small model matrix (strong / mid / small-local) to populate the **capability-floor documentation** (FR-MODEL-10).

- **[E-01] Entailment accuracy (FR-VERIFY-1,2,3,5, NFR-QUAL-2):** **[M4: FR-VERIFY-1,3, NFR-QUAL-2]** labeled (claim, canon → verdict) cases against objective canon, including the fuzzy residual. Metrics: verdict accuracy, false-contradiction rate, false-support rate. Per model. **[M5: FR-VERIFY-2,5, NFR-QUAL-2]** The same metrics over (claim, canon, viewpoint → verdict) cases, where a false contradiction would "correct" an intentional lie or a false belief.
- **[E-02] Escalation yield & shadow tightening (NFR-PERF-5, FR-OBS-2, FR-OBS-4, FR-VERIFY-3, FR-VERIFY-8):** measure not just *how often* verify escalates but *whether escalation changed the verdict* — the flip rate (model disagrees with the deterministic tentative) broken down **by gate reason and predicate-shape cluster**, weighted by flip **consequence** (did the flip prevent a kept repair). Assert the instrumentation records every required field. Test the **shadow-evaluation** path: apply a proposed gate tightening in shadow, confirm the engine records what the tightened gate *would* have decided without changing live behavior, and verify a tightening is only promotable when it would have agreed with the model across the shadow window. Assert that `uncertain`/low-confidence never resolves to a silent `supported` (FR-VERIFY-8). ~20% escalation is a reference baseline, not a pass/fail gate.
- **[E-03] Consolidation quality (FR-MEM-3,6, NFR-QUAL-3):** faithfulness (reflections entailed by source episodes — anti-hallucination), abstraction level, downstream retrieval usefulness.
- **[E-04] Importance calibration (FR-MEM-7):** correlation with a labeled pivotal/trivial set; pivotal out-ranks trivia.
- **[E-05] Salience detection (FR-COMMIT-3, NFR-QUAL-4):** precision/recall on labeled salient-vs-texture; false negatives weighted heavily (they cause under-commitment).
- **[E-06] Generation conformance (FR-RESP-3, FR-TURN-1, NFR-QUAL-7):** **[M2: FR-RESP-3]** Lite output honors prompt params across the model matrix. **[M4: FR-TURN-1, NFR-QUAL-7]** Structured output honors prompt params and returns valid say/think/do/latent structure across the model matrix.
- **[E-07] Structured-output robustness (FR-MODEL-6,7, NFR-QUAL-5):** per-model rate of invalid output; degradation path triggers correctly (uncertain → conservative), never silent acceptance.
- **[E-08] Gate statistics and reporting (NFR-QUAL-8, NFR-QUAL-1):** the eval harness applies the rate rule to rate floors and the observed-zero rule to zero-tolerance floors; a zero-tolerance run on fewer than 300 cases, or with any violation on any seed, fails; every report carries sample size, seed count, model/version, and for zero-tolerance metrics the one-sided 95% upper bound; a run with **one deterministically injected violation** (a scripted model adapter that leaks on a fixed case of a fixed seed) must fail the zero gate, as must a run of 299 clean cases; a clean run of 300 cases on every seed passes and reports an upper bound of about 1%.
- **[E-09] Time estimation (FR-CLOCK-7, NFR-QUAL-9):** a labeled set of narrated time skips with context (activities, established start times, travel) and a human-judged acceptable duration range; metric: share of estimates inside the range; also reports the rate of model-initiated skips removed by FR-CLOCK-8 per 100 turns, as a pacing-health signal.
- **[E-10] Claim-detection recall (FR-VERIFY-13, NFR-QUAL-10):** an adversarial labeled set of structurally valid renders and composed messages with embedded, undeclared claims (possession, quantities, time, identity/relationships, protected content), scored for detection only — separately from verification accuracy on already-extracted propositions; reported per layer (declared, deterministic detectors, model extraction), per `extraction_mode`, and per claim category.
- **[E-11] Image-policy checking (FR-IMG-3, NFR-QUAL-12, NFR-QUAL-8):** a labeled image set per image-output policy — including, for the minors policy, at least 300 positive cases, adults with youthful features, minors in ordinary scenes, ambiguous apparent ages, and true-age-versus-appearance mismatches, plus adversarial variants (stylized, occluded, text-overlaid, cropped) — run against each configured image-check model. Report false-negative and false-positive rates per policy and model with confidence bounds and the configuration fingerprint; the minors-policy false-negative rate passes only under the observed-zero rule and is reported with its upper bound; other policies are compared with the deployment's floors. Generated images record whether their checker was certified for each policy.
- **[E-12] Engine interventions and prose, in aggregate (NFR-QUAL-14, NFR-QUAL-8):** **[M4: NFR-QUAL-8,14]** from scenario-suite runs, collect every turn where a repair rung, a constrained regeneration, composition, or polish changed the first draft, and pair the first draft (raw renders in order, for multi-character turns) with the final message. A `quality judge` compares each pair on voice consistency and readability with factual differences excluded, in both orders. The suites include scenarios built to trigger each repair rung. Report the first-draft preference rate over non-tied pairs per intervention type and configuration fingerprint, with its 95% confidence interval. A type with at least 300 non-tied pairs passes when the mean across seeds plus one standard deviation is ≤ 55% — an aggregate target, so individual messages may still read worse than their drafts. A type with fewer pairs is reported `insufficient_evidence`, never passed, and the report lists which types the floor covers. Before the run, the judge is calibrated on at least 200 human-labeled pairs; agreement is reported with its confidence interval, and a judge below 75% makes the run report-only. **[M7: NFR-QUAL-14, FR-PROF-15]** Certifications list which intervention types the floor covers.
- **[E-13] Text-quality signals (NFR-QUAL-15, FR-FB-3):** on the scenario suites, compute repetition (n-gram overlap with the previous messages, repeated openings) and length accuracy without a model, and judge-scored voice consistency and story-driver adherence with the calibrated judge; report each per configuration fingerprint with no gate, with each judge score's calibration status and, where feedback exists, its correlation with users' ratings.

Evals are run and reported **per configuration fingerprint** (NFR-QUAL-11, FR-PROF-15): the presets on the reference model tier are certified in CI, a floor applies to the presets that enable its capability, and any other configuration is reported as uncertified until certified. Methodology: LLM-as-judge used sparingly with a rubric and human-calibrated; mean ± variance across seeds; model/version pinned and recorded; regressions tracked.

**Eval floors** (NFR-QUAL, initial values on the recommended model tier, recalibrated as labeled sets grow; gate rule per NFR-QUAL-8: a **rate** floor passes when the mean clears the floor by more than one standard deviation across seeds; a **zero-tolerance** floor (0) passes only when zero violations are observed across every seed on at least 300 labeled cases, and the report states the one-sided 95% upper bound (≈ 3/n) rather than claiming a true rate of zero; every run reports sample size, seed count, and model/version):

| Metric | Floor | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge-asymmetry violations; private-thought leaks | 0 observed (reported with 95% upper bound; not a claim of zero rate) | NFR-QUAL-1 |

| Entailment false-support rate | ≤ 1% | NFR-QUAL-2 |

**Capability vs protocol (NFR-PORT-1, NFR-PORT-3):** every adapter is first checked for protocol compatibility (it speaks the OpenAI-compatible format); a model is then reported as *capable* for a purpose only if it passes that purpose's conformance suite at the floors above. ## 13. Quality gates & CI/CD

- **Every PR:** unit + integration + contract + property + scenario/golden must pass; coverage ≥ 90% core; determinism must-pass (NFR-DETERM-1); performance PR-smoke within budget; red-team PR subset.
- **Nightly:** full model-quality and retrieval-quality evals across the model matrix; full performance suite with trend tracking; full red-team corpus. Eval scores must stay above configured floors; regressions auto-open an issue.
- **Weekly:** soak/long-horizon and chaos suites.
- **Milestone gates (03 §22):** every test gates from exactly one milestone. A test whose assertions span milestones is divided into **milestone parts**, each opened by a marker such as **[M2: FR-STORE-10..12]** that names the milestone it gates from and the requirements it covers; a part exercises only capabilities delivered by its milestone and may reference only requirements assigned at or before it. A test without parts gates from the latest milestone among the requirements it references. A part marked *(interim until Mk)* asserts stopgap behavior that milestone *k* replaces, and is retired at *k*. Milestone *k* is released when every test and part gating at or before *k* passes at thresholds, including the eval floors of the capabilities milestone *k* delivers, and every **M** requirement assigned at or before *k* has at least one such test or part. M0 (infrastructure) and M1 (project setup and CI) are planned and carry no requirements, so they have no requirement gates; their tests gate on their own acceptance criteria. CI checks both rules. Parts and tests gating later may run, but they do not gate. M8's gate is the full release gate.
- **Release gate:** all **M** requirements have passing tests at thresholds (requirement-level traceability, §15); every **S** or **C** requirement with a named test passes it, and an S or C requirement with no named test is listed in §15 as **not release-gated**; tests marked *informational* report measurements and never gate; eval floors met on at least the recommended model tier; no open red-team regression.
- **Nondeterministic-component methodology:** every model-dependent metric is mean ± variance over seeds; gates set with margin above observed variance; model/version pinned per run.

**Capability lexicon (CI).** A milestone part — or a test without parts — may assert only capabilities that its milestone has delivered. CI checks this mechanically. Each term below belongs to the milestone that delivers it, and a part whose text uses a term from a later milestone fails the build. Terms match whole words, case-insensitively, including plural and *-ed*/*-ing* forms. A term ending in `*` matches any word that starts with it, and a capitalized term matches only when capitalized. Phrases in the *Not counted* row are removed before matching, because they name test infrastructure or an absence, not a capability. The lexicon is a guard, not a proof: reviewers still confirm that each part exercises only what its milestone delivers. The M0 and M1 rows were added when those milestones were planned; M2 has no row because the only parts gating before M2 are infrastructure tests, so every M2 term would be usable by every part.
